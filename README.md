## `background`

### 🖥️ System Hell (SysAdmin)

> My baseline is years of grueling combat against infrastructure collapsing under its own weight. Some people call this a job; I call it a punishment for curiosity.
> 
> What consumes my mind every single day:
> * Keeping Linux servers alive on life support
> * Endless fixes for volatile access controls (permission hell)
> * Bypassing and patching broken security constraints
> * Configuring networks that are inherently insecure by design
> * Fighting malignant configurations that break the moment the wind blows
> * Automating everything just to minimize human contact and hypocrisy
> 
> This experience taught me one absolute truth: no system works reliably. If you think everything is stable, it just means you haven't looked closely enough at the logs.

---

### 🔎 OSINT (The Art of Digital Stalking)

> I despise the very concept of privacy in a world where everyone leaves digital tracks and naively believes their secrets are safe. Humans are always the weakest link in any security architecture.
> 
> * Deep data correlation and artifact harvesting from open sources
> * Unearthing information people tried to bury in the deepest corners of memory
> * Analyzing metadata that reveals far more about you than your words ever could
> * Infrastructure reconnaissance and network mapping
> * Proving once and for all that internet anonymity is a fairy-tale myth

### ⚙️ Reverse Engineering

> Binaries, assembly language, and hexadecimal code are the only pure forms of art left in this world. Code tells the truth, unlike human promises. If a function throws an error, it does so without a fake smile.
> 
> * Static and dynamic analysis of garbage software
> * Working with x86/x64 architectures and parsing raw instructions
> * Obfuscation, deobfuscation, and power-debugging
> * Hunting for logical flaws, vulnerabilities, and logic bombs inside binaries
> * Researching how to force a program to do the exact things its creators feared most
